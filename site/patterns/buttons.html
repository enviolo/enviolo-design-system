Buttons

How buttons combine on real screens: which variant to pick, and examples from Enviolo products.

Choosing a type

Ask in this order and stop at the first yes.

  1. Is it the main thing people come here to do?Primary. Only one per view.
  2. Does it support the main job and change something?Secondary on plain backgrounds, outline on cards, dialogs and imagery.
  3. Is it repeated, optional or a way out?Ghost.
  4. Is it navigation inside a sentence?Link. Irreversible instead? Destructive, next to an outline way out.

Variants in use

One job each. Default ranks first; secondary and outline share rank 2, and the surface decides which one.

VariantUse forAvoid
DefaultThe main job of the view, like Find a dealer, Place order or Start course.More than one per view. If two actions feel equally important, the view is doing two jobs.
SecondarySupporting actions on plain backgrounds, filters and toggles, and a second action with a consequence.Cards, dialogs and imagery, where the muted fill blends into the surface.
OutlineSupporting actions on cards, panels, dialogs and imagery, and the safe way out of a dialog.Full-width buttons in mobile forms, where a bordered pill reads like an empty input.
GhostRepeated actions on rows and toolbars, skip or defer actions, and icon buttons.The only action on a screen, or anything people must not miss.
LinkNavigation inside a sentence.Standalone actions. If it does something, it should be a button.
DestructiveIrreversible actions, always next to an outline way out.Anything that can be undone, or as the default focus.

Rules of thumb

One primary per view

If two actions feel equally important, the view is doing two jobs. Split it.

Primary goes last

Right-aligned on desktop footers, top of the stack on mobile.

Surface picks rank 2

Secondary on plain backgrounds, outline on cards, dialogs and imagery. Never both in one group.

Repeated means ghost

Anything shown on every row, card or toolbar slot drops to ghost.

Name the outcome

"Place order", "Download manual", not "Submit" or "OK". Icon-only buttons always get a label for screen readers.

Destructive is its own variant

Only for irreversible actions, and always next to an outline way out.
